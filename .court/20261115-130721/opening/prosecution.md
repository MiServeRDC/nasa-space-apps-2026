# Opening for the Prosecution

The claim on trial is that ARES-Health "should be submitted and pitched exactly as it currently stands." It should not. Five concrete failures.

**1. The Fresh Code rule may disqualify the entry outright.** The rule: "ALL submitted application code must be original and written during the hackathon weekend (48 hours)... Proprietary or pre-built codebases must not be imported." Against this stands "8 Rust crates, native OS shell, custom forecaster, DSP, knowledge graph" and the admission that "the record does not state how much was written inside the official window versus before it." If an eighth of this scope predates the window, the cost is not points but disqualification. Submitting "as-is" without resolving this gambles the whole entry.

**2. The core deliverable is self-labelled unvalidated.** The challenge is astronaut health decisions; the project states its "scenario-derived physiology, risk/readiness scores and forecast ranges are 'illustrative and clinically unvalidated,'" with calibration and outcome claims "not established." Validity/Scientific Soundness is 20% of the score and directly measures this; the gap also drags Impact and Relevance. A judge reading the project's own banner cannot score Validity high.

**3. The scope cannot be demonstrated in the allowed format.** The demo "must fit" a "strictly 30-second video" or "maximum 7-slide" deck. Microsecond latencies, audit chains, ChaCha20-Poly1305, three safety gates and a Tauri OS shell are unverifiable in 30 seconds and read as "scope that cannot be demonstrated or verified in a short pitch." The built depth converts to zero demonstrated value under the hard limit.

**4. The pitch artifact is absent from the record.** The claim is "pitched exactly as it currently stands," yet the case file describes no completed video or deck — only "Presentation/Storytelling" as a 20% axis. One-fifth of the score rests on a deliverable the record does not show exists.

**5. The team lacks the recommended clinical voice.** "NASA recommends multidisciplinary teams (... a clinical storyteller/pitch producer)." The record lists "a lead architect who is an M.Sc. computer-engineering student... plus four others," none identified as clinical. Four of five axes are not engineering; the team is weighted to the one that is.
