# Rebuttal for the Defense

**1. Fresh Code — does not survive as disqualification.** The rule permits pre-existing open-source libraries, public datasets, and pre-prepared architecture specs and formulas as planning documents; the record says only that it "does not state how much was written inside the window" — silence, not proof of a violation. A disclosure to resolve, not grounds to pull the entry.

**2. Unvalidated core — survives.** The challenge is astronaut health decisions, Validity is 20%, and the project's own banner caps how high a judge can score it. The honesty and the stated subject-disjoint calibration path mitigate, but do not cure.

**3. Scope vs. format — does not survive.** Every entrant faces the same 30-second/7-slide limit; the thesis — offline edge is the only physically valid architecture under an 8-to-48-minute delay — lands in one sentence. A pitch-editing choice, not a defect.

**4. Missing pitch artifact — survives partially.** The record shows no finished video or deck, so "pitched as it stands" is unproven; but a producible deliverable missing from the record is a readiness gap, not a failure of the project itself.

**5. No clinical voice — does not survive.** The record lists "four others" without stating their disciplines; "none identified as clinical" is absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.

**The jury should decide Point 2:** whether a system that openly labels its clinical models unvalidated can still be globally competitive for a challenge about astronaut health decisions. That is the one charge the record lets them weigh on the merits, not on silence.
